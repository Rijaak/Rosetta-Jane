# Contributors

## Author

**Rijaak** is the author of Rosetta-Jane. Rijaak conceived it, commissioned the work, directed its design at every step, wrote parts of its wording, chose what stayed and what went, and claims copyright in it as its author.

What is Rijaak's own, specifically:

- the concept: one model split across many devices with no central server, "like Napster", as the opposite of an offline box;
- group codes, in the manner of a co-op game lobby, alongside an open public network;
- splitting the model so that no one device holds the whole picture;
- catching dishonest peers by having several do the same work, and by testing them with work whose answer is already known;
- switching that checking on whenever three peers are present, not only on the public network;
- keeping known answers locked to the device that made them, so a group cannot pass them around;
- training through the swarm, built into the same program instead of a separate tool;
- the rule that a trained adapter is never applied automatically, that this is a deliberate speed bump, and that it is a requirement and not a request;
- a fingerprint of every handoff between devices;
- the warning shown before the p2p service starts, largely in Rijaak's words;
- a phone app that is a full peer, not a lesser client;
- the names: Rosetta-Jane, and "p2p service" for what a device offers the group;
- the choice of licence and the rule about who may use the name.

The name comes from Jane, the character in Orson Scott Card's Ender series.
