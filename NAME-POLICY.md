# Who may call their software Rosetta-Jane

The code of Rosetta-Jane is free to use, change and share under its licence. The **name** is a separate matter.
The name tells people what they are getting, and in particular that the safeguards below are in place. This
policy says who may use it. It rests on trademark rights in the name, not on the software licence.

## The safeguards the name stands for

1. **Trained adapters are never applied automatically.** A person downloads one adapter at a time, sees how much
   of its training was checked, and applies it themselves.
2. **The warning comes first.** Before a device starts the p2p service, its owner is shown what that involves
   and has to acknowledge it.
3. **Honest reporting.** Work that was checked and work that was not are reported as such.
4. **Consent.** A device only takes part when its owner starts the program, and only in the group whose code its
   owner entered. Nothing joins a device to a network silently.
5. **Open source.** The source code of the version people are running is available to them.

## What you may do

- Use the name **Rosetta-Jane** for unmodified copies of this project.
- Use the name for a modified version that keeps all five safeguards, as long as you make clear it is your
  version and not the original (for example "Rosetta-Jane, Example Labs build").
- Say truthfully that your software is "based on Rosetta-Jane", "compatible with Rosetta-Jane" or "works with
  Rosetta-Jane", whatever you have changed.

## What you may not do

- Call a version **Rosetta-Jane**, or anything likely to be confused with it, if it removes or weakens any of
  the five safeguards. Give it a different name. You may still say what it was based on.
- Suggest that Rijaak or the Rosetta-Jane project made, approved or supports your version when that is not so.
- Use the name for a service or product in a way that suggests it is the official one.

## Why this exists

The licence keeps every version's code open, so anyone can see what a version does. It does not stop someone
publishing a version with the safeguards taken out. This policy means such a version cannot borrow the trust
that goes with the name.

## Its limits

The name is not a registered trademark at the time of writing. Rights in an unregistered name come from using
it publicly and are narrower and harder to enforce than a registration. If the project grows, registering the
name is the step that gives this policy real force. This is not legal advice.

Questions about using the name: open an issue on the project's repository.
